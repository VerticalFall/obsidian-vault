> the race is not inevitable
> 
> last year was fun.
> 
> the models weren't that good yet, so i had to care about every detail. i pushed each thing until it had my thoughts and my designs. the tool helped. i did the driving.
> 
> now the roles have flipped. we describe and watch things come back finished. we used to drive our dreams. more and more, we just watch.
> 
> we set out to make things that give us more freedom. now we're racing toward something we might not be able to control.
> 
> recursive self-improvement. intelligence building more intelligence, until we're no longer the ones who understand what's happening.
> 
> that's the end game. the point where nobody is driving.
> 
> the big labs are racing to get there first. the same system that cures a disease could design a new virus, or find every hole in the internet all at once. only the selected few with access could do anything about it.
> 
> and somehow the possibility of losing everything we love becomes an argument for going faster.
> 
> because someone else might get there first, and they might be less careful.
> because the company needs to survive.
> because too much money is already committed.
> 
> everyone has a good reason to keep going toward an outcome nobody wants. so we call it inevitable. then nobody has to answer for it.
> 
> but a race is made of decisions. someone approves the budget. someone decides the next model will help build the one after it.
> 
> and we practice living inside the race long before we build it at the scale of civilization.
> 
> sleep becomes recovery for work. friendship becomes a network. curiosity has to become a project before we let ourselves spend time on it.
> 
> we get very rational. we start acting like the machines we're building.
> 
> even our feelings have to defend themselves.
> why are you sad? is that useful?
> 
> sometimes the feeling is how i begin to find out.
> 
> there's a line in Zhuangzi.
> 大知閑閑，小知閒閒。
> 
> great knowledge is wide and unhurried. small knowledge is busy, always measuring.
> 
> i don't think you can hurry your way to the first kind.
> 
> i moved to Taipei to slow down and experience life.
> 
> walking around Da'an. going into a cafe, or a cheap buffet. meeting new friends by accident, who brought me to a shisha bar. a 24-hour tea house, talking about life and the world we're in. getting closer to someone and feeling my heart beat faster...
> 
> none of it was planned. none of it would show up in a report.
> 
> i don't want to become so certain about where i'm going that nothing can reach me along the way.
> 
> there's a tree in Zhuangzi too. a carpenter walks past a giant oak and calls it useless wood.
> 
> that night the tree comes to him in a dream. the fruit trees you call useful get stripped every year, it says. being of no use is how i got to grow this big.
> 
> the race looks at the world the way the carpenter looks at the tree. a night in a tea house is useless wood. so is a feeling you can't explain yet.
> 
> that's most of what i want to protect.
> 
> and there's a cook. after nineteen years his knife moves fast and easy, because he follows the openings between the joints. but at a hard knot, he slows down. when it's done, he stands there holding the knife and looks around.
> 
> the people building these systems are some of the best cooks alive. this is the hardest knot anyone has come to. it's where a master slows down.
> 
> we cloned a sheep thirty years ago, and we still don't clone people.
> 
> we can slow down. change direction. decide something isn't worth building, even if it would make someone very rich.
> 
> a probability can help us think about a risk. it can't decide what we have the right to risk on behalf of everyone else.
> 
> i want what we build to stay answerable to the people who have to live with it. most of them will never be in the room.
> 
> there are no stands in this race. whatever happens, happens to us.
> 
> i'd like the people in that room to come out and sit for a while. it's harder to gamble with ordinary life once you've spent an evening in the middle of it.
> 
> the tea house is open all night.
